# VIVA.md: questions I expect, with answers

These were written during the build, as each decision was made. Wherever an answer quotes a number, the number comes from `results/metrics.json`, which is regenerated by `python -m src.evaluate --report`.

---

### Data

**Q1. Your data is synthetic. Why should I believe any of your numbers?**
The synthetic numbers show that the *pipeline* is correct: a time-based split, honest baselines, calibrated confidence, and a detector scored against known ground truth. They don't claim real-world accuracy. That claim comes from the **real validation set**: 300 real bank-statement strings labelled by three people, plus 30 anchor strings that all three labelled so I could measure how much humans agree with each other. The gap between the synthetic and real scores is reported as a finding. I also wrote my expected real-data accuracy in DECISIONS.md *before* seeing the labels, and committed it first, so git history proves I didn't move the goalposts.

**Q2. How did you stop the generator from making the task trivially easy?**
Three things. First, shared platforms: Amazon Pay, Paytm, Swiggy and others appear under several categories. Second, payments to individuals, whose names come from one shared pool across categories. An auto driver and a kirana owner look identical on a statement. Third, surface noise: legal entity names, truncation, typos and casing. On top of that there's a gate. If synthetic accuracy is above 92%, the training script prints `LEAKAGE SUSPECTED`, exits with an error, and I make the *generator* harder. I never tune the model toward a target number.

**Q3. Why mask digits?**
Privacy and robustness. Real strings contain phone numbers and account references. Every run of ≥4 digits becomes `#` before the string is written to any file. The model applies the same mask to all input, so it can't learn from reference numbers, which are random and carry no category signal anyway.
